# Buying nuances

## Three quantities, not one

- **Bridge:** the smallest quantity that covers demand until the next supply is
  expected to land.
- **Recommended:** target cover, rounded to cartons and lifted to MOQ.
- **Take-all:** everything the supplier has. Only meaningful when the offer gives
  an exact quantity. "100+" says at least 100 exist and nothing about how many
  more.

Respect carton multiples and minimums in all three. Say which constraint set
each quantity.

## Longer cover

Longer cover makes sense when the supplier says stock will not return for a
while, the lead time is long, or a price break is worth it. Weigh it against
cash tied up, shelf or warehouse space, shelf life, the risk of the line being
superseded, and orders already inbound.

## Demand is not always what sales say

- **Stockouts suppress sales.** Weeks at zero stock should not count as zero
  demand. Use weeks in stock, or a comparable period, and say which.
- **Promotions inflate sales.** A promoted week is not a normal week. Strip it
  out or name it.
- **One snapshot proves little.** Zero stock today proves neither a long outage
  nor a coming restock.
- **New lines have no history.** Buying one is a ranging decision. That a
  supplier has stock is not a reason to range it.

## Offer history

If you have earlier offers from the same supplier, compare them: restocks,
depletions, cost changes, ETA changes, lines added and lines dropped. A line
missing from a partial offer is not discontinued, and a long supplier outage is
not your lead time.

## Costs

- Price breaks change the unit cost between options. Recalculate cost for each
  quantity; do not carry one option's unit cost to another.
- Keep tax basis consistent. Australian trade costs are usually ex GST.
- Freight, duty and exchange make the difference between invoice cost and landed
  cost. Name which you are quoting.
- For a foreign currency, quote the original amount and an indicative
  conversion with the rate and its date. An indicative rate is not what the bank
  will settle at.
- Never sum or budget across mixed currencies. Convert first, and say so.

## Highlighted or annotated rows

Suppliers colour rows or add notes such as "discontinued", "last chance" or
"pre-order". Read them as the supplier's notes and ask the buyer what the
colours mean if it is not stated. A "discontinued" line is a risk to check, not
proof.
